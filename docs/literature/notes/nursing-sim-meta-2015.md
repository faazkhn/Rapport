# Shin 2015: Effectiveness of patient simulation in nursing education: meta-analysis

- **Citation:** Shin S, Park JH, Kim JH. Nurse Education Today 35(1):176-182, 2015. DOI 10.1016/j.nedt.2014.09.009
- **Link:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0260691714003074 · **Open access:** abstract only (via repository abstracts)
- **Code:** none · **Code licence:** none
- **Paper licence:** publisher
- **Informs:** feedback/pedagogy, evaluation

## Mechanism
Random-effects meta-analysis of patient-simulation studies in nursing education (EBSCO, Medline, ScienceDirect, ERIC).

## Evidence
2,503 articles screened, 20 included. Pooled SMD 0.71 (medium-large). Larger effects for performance outcomes, psychomotor skills, clinical nurses/senior students, high-fidelity simulators.

## Limitations
- Nursing, mostly manikin simulators, not conversational agents.
- Abstract only.
- Heterogeneous outcomes.

## For Rapport
**Verdict:** skip. Background justification for simulation training only; no mechanism for the Digital Twin.
