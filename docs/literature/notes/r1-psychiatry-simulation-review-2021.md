# Simulation Training in Psychiatry for Medical Education: A Review

- **Citation:** Piot M-A, Attoe C, Billon G, et al., Frontiers in Psychiatry 12:658967, 2021. DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2021.658967
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2021.658967 · **Open access:** yes (full text read, local PDF)
- **Code:** none · **Code licence:** none (not software)
- **Paper licence:** CC BY (Frontiers open access)
- **Informs:** feedback/pedagogy, behaviour/resistance, identity/formulation

## Mechanism
A narrative (non-systematic) review drawing on historical simulation texts, SBE guidelines and a wider systematic review by the authors. It covers the history (Barrows 1963, Rosenhan, ASPE), learning theories (Kolb, Bandura, social constructionism, Stanislavsky's "state of I am"), the three-phase session (pre-brief, scenario, debrief) and a table of technologies (role play, simulated patient, manikin, VR), then the opportunities and the psychiatry-specific controversies.

## Evidence
No quantitative results; no pooled data. Argument-based. Key claims:
- Authentic (indistinguishable from real) is not the same as valid; a caricatured portrayal lacking ambivalence can look authentic to a novice but not to an experienced psychiatrist.
- Complex presentations (schizophrenia, mania) played by novices risk caricature; role play suits easier ones (typical depression).
- Real patients do not plan how they present: they may be uncertain or resistant even to themselves, and the clinician must build rapport by "feeling ahead".
- Scripted standardisation (needed for exams) conflicts with flexible, valid portrayal; recommended target is "good enough" portrayal, not perfect depiction.
- Stereotyping risk when scenarios follow epidemiology (e.g. the white middle-class woman with an eating disorder); involve real patients.
- Fiction versus reality blurs for pretence-related conditions (as-if personalities, malingering); VR risks "hyper-reality".
- Debrief rules: psychological safety, Socratic questioning, trained facilitator.

## Limitations
- Narrative review, 2021, pre-LLM; virtual patients get only a few sentences.
- No effect sizes or search method in the paper itself.
- Mostly human SP and role-play practice.

## For Rapport
**Verdict:** adapt (design principles, no code). Use the authentic-vs-valid distinction as an evaluation criterion (ask psychiatrists, not students, to judge fidelity), keep ambivalence and resistance in the case formulation rather than a single prototype, vary demographics across cases to avoid stereotypes, and make the debrief Socratic and psychologically safe. Supports the "feel first, speak second" brain over scripted answers: rigid scripts fail validity.
