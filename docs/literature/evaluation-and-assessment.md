# Evaluating the learner: what the evidence says, and what Rapport now does

**Date:** 2026-10-25. **Scope:** how Rapport scores and gives feedback on a learner's interview (the debrief), not how it evaluates the patient simulator (that is `insights.md` and `PATIENTS.md`).
**Sources read for this pass:** the existing 83 notes in `notes/` (feedback and rubric papers re-read), the Awesome-LLM-Patient-Simulators list (FreedomIntelligence, evaluation entries), the MIRS LLM-scoring benchmark, two meta-analyses or reviews, OSCE standard-setting papers, and the clinical guidelines below. Clinical statements stay **draft until faculty review**, like the cases.

## Summary

1. **Score the MSE against what the patient visibly showed, and penalise recording what was not there.** The old engine marked only 5 "required" cues, ignored the case's other displayed signs (Arjun's nail-picking) and gave full marks however many wrong boxes were ticked. Recording signs that are absent is an observation error examiners mark down; a sign that never appeared in a session cannot fairly be required.
2. **Risk is assessed, not stratified.** NICE NG225 (2022) says not to use risk tools or global low/medium/high labels to predict suicide or decide care. A psychosocial assessment covers ideation, intent, plan, means, past behaviour and protective factors, done compassionately. Rapport scores each risk item, credits disclosure, and never prints a risk category.
3. **Asking is not the same as finding out.** A risk question asked before the patient trusts the doctor gets a deflection. Partial credit plus "come back to it" teaches the real skill (C-SSRS: ask directly, build up from wish-to-be-dead to plan and behaviour).
4. **Case-specific critical items.** In alcohol dependence, a past withdrawal seizure or delirium means medically assisted withdrawal must be supervised (NICE CG100 1.1.1.1 "high risk of withdrawal seizures or delirium tremens"; IPS CPG). Missing it is as unsafe as missing suicide.
5. **Communication from validated anchors, in code.** MIRS (the Master Interview Rating Scale, used in the MedSimAI and Geathers studies) and Calgary-Cambridge items: opening, open questions, empathy, jargon, one question at a time, summarising, checking understanding. LLM scorers are weakest exactly on "minimises jargon" and non-verbal items (Geathers 2025), so jargon and stacked questions are counted by code.
6. **A global rating next to the checklist.** OSCEs pair a checklist with an examiner's global judgement. Checklists anchor pass/fail; global ratings help at the borderline. Critical items must be able to fail a candidate whatever the total (the Vietnamese cardiovascular OSCE found critical items under-weighted, so candidates could miss them and pass).
7. **Feedback learners can act on.** Learners rated actionable feedback 4.7/5 against avatar realism 3.4/5 (SOPHIE RCT). Structured feedback improved history taking in an RCT (Brügge 2024, small). MedSimAI: quote-based rubric items. Rapport now leads the debrief with the 2-3 highest-stakes changes, each with words to try (R2C2 / Pendleton: what went well, then what to do next time), and quotes the learner's own lines.
8. **AI examiner notes only as a second opinion.** On 28 MIRS items, LLMs matched expert scores exactly only 27-44% of the time, within one point 67-87%, and best at flagging weak performance (thresholded 75-88%). Zero-shot beat chain-of-thought and few-shot. Non-verbal items were unreliable (Gemini alpha -0.47). So: zero-shot, temperature 0, verbal items only, never part of the score, and every quote checked against the transcript in code.

## What the debrief now does (ScoringEngine)

Weights are unchanged (risk 30, observation 20, history 12, communication 12, diagnosis 13, management 13; plan C1).

- **Risk (30).** Items: risk intents plus the case's `critical_intents`. Each item: asked and answered 1, asked but not answered 0.6, not asked 0 and a critical error. "Answered" means a must-ask fact it unlocks was disclosed.
- **Observation (20).** Expected cues = the case's required and MSE-expected cues that were visible for at least 5 s this session (intensity >= 0.35 while seated), plus the tremor when the hands were held out. Recall 16, precision 4 (a ticked sign that is not part of this patient costs). Expected signs that never appeared are listed "not shown this session" and cost nothing.
- **Communication (12).** 60% from six items (introduction, confidentiality, open questions at least a quarter of turns, two or more empathy statements, summarising, checking understanding), 40% from final trust; minus 4 per judgmental line or real interruption (now only when at least 1 s of the patient's reply was cut off), up to 2 for jargon and 1.5 for stacked questions.
- **Global rating.** Excellent >= 80, clear pass >= 65, borderline pass >= 50, otherwise borderline fail; one critical error caps it at borderline fail, two or more at clear fail. Provisional until faculty run a borderline-regression standard set.
- **Next time, focus on.** The three highest-stakes changes, critical first, each with an example phrase from `intents.json` (`example`).
- **Examiner notes (AI, formative).** `POST /assess` (`examiner.py`): eight verbal items, up to three strengths and three improvements, every quote checked against what the doctor said; shown below the score and never added to it.

## Gaps and open items

- NICE CG100's full text was not reachable (HTTP 403); its recommendation 1.1.1.1 is quoted from NICE's guidance page summary and secondary sources. Faculty should confirm the alcohol case against the current IPS alcohol guideline.
- The MIRS 28-item anchors were not retrieved (held by the University of Connecticut and Tennessee); Rapport uses the item names reported in the benchmark papers, not the official anchors.
- Global-rating cut-offs are provisional; a borderline-regression standard needs a real cohort.
- The AI examiner is validated only against the literature's general findings, not against our faculty. Run it beside two faculty raters on 20 sessions before trusting it for anything summative.
- Non-verbal communication (eye contact, posture of the learner) is not assessed: the learner is not on camera, and the literature says current models cannot do it reliably.
